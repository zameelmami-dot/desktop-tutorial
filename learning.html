Learning

How experience changes what we know and how we behave.

പഠനം — അനുഭവങ്ങളിലൂടെ അറിവിലും പെരുമാറ്റത്തിലും ഉണ്ടാകുന്ന മാറ്റം.

Learning outcomes

After studying this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define learning and identify its main characteristics.
  • Distinguish learning from temporary changes in behaviour.
  • Explain classical conditioning, operant conditioning and observational learning.
  • Identify factors that influence learning and apply them to everyday situations.

1. What is learning?

Definition: Learning is a relatively enduring change in behaviour or knowledge that occurs as a result of experience or practice.

മലയാളത്തിൽ: അനുഭവം, പരിശീലനം എന്നിവയുടെ ഫലമായി അറിവിലോ പെരുമാറ്റത്തിലോ ഉണ്ടാകുന്ന താരതമ്യേന സ്ഥിരതയുള്ള മാറ്റമാണ് പഠനം.
Simple example: A student practises typing every day. Over time, the student types faster and makes fewer mistakes. This improvement through practice is learning.

Important words:

  • Change: Something becomes different — for example, a new skill is acquired.
  • Relatively enduring: The change is not just momentary; it lasts beyond a brief temporary state.
  • Experience or practice: The change develops through interaction, observation, feedback or practice.

Learning may not always be directly visible. A person can learn something but may not show it until there is an opportunity or motivation to perform.

2. Characteristics of learning

1. Learning brings changeIt may change knowledge, skills, attitudes or behaviour.
2. It comes through experiencePractice, observation and interaction can produce learning.
3. It is relatively lastingIt is more than a brief, temporary change.
4. It can be positive or negativePeople can learn helpful habits as well as unhelpful habits.
5. It occurs throughout lifeLearning can take place at home, college, work and in society.
6. It may be intentional or unintentionalWe learn through planned study and everyday experiences.
ഓർക്കുക: പഠനം എന്നത് പുസ്തകത്തിൽ നിന്ന് അറിവ് നേടുന്നതിൽ മാത്രം ഒതുങ്ങുന്നില്ല. പുതിയ കഴിവുകൾ, ശീലങ്ങൾ, മനോഭാവങ്ങൾ എന്നിവയും അനുഭവത്തിലൂടെ പഠിക്കാം.

4. Classical conditioning — Ivan Pavlov

Meaning: Classical conditioning is learning through association. A previously neutral stimulus comes to produce a response after being repeatedly paired with a stimulus that naturally produces that response.

Pavlov studied digestion in dogs. He noticed that dogs began to salivate not only when food was presented, but also in response to signals associated with food.

Everyday example: Imagine a student repeatedly hears the college bell just before lunch. After some time, the bell itself may create an expectation of lunch.

Key terms

TermMeaning in Pavlov's experiment
Unconditioned stimulus (UCS)Food — naturally produces salivation.
Unconditioned response (UCR)Salivation to food — natural response.
Neutral stimulus (NS)Bell before conditioning — initially does not produce the target salivation response.
Conditioned stimulus (CS)Bell after it has been paired with food.
Conditioned response (CR)Salivation in response to the bell.
ലളിതമായി: ഭക്ഷണം കണ്ടാൽ നായയ്ക്ക് സ്വാഭാവികമായി ഉമിനീർ വരും. ഭക്ഷണം നൽകുന്നതിന് മുമ്പ് മണി ആവർത്തിച്ച് മുഴക്കിയപ്പോൾ, പിന്നീട് മണി കേട്ടാലും നായയ്ക്ക് ഉമിനീർ വരാൻ തുടങ്ങി. ഇതാണ് Classical Conditioning.

Other important processes

  • Acquisition: The association is learned.
  • Extinction: The conditioned response weakens when the conditioned stimulus is repeatedly presented without the original unconditioned stimulus.
  • Spontaneous recovery: An extinguished response may reappear after a rest period.
  • Stimulus generalisation: Similar stimuli may produce a similar conditioned response.
  • Stimulus discrimination: Learning to respond differently to distinct stimuli.

5. Operant conditioning — B. F. Skinner

Meaning: Operant conditioning is learning in which the consequences of a behaviour influence how likely that behaviour is to occur again.

Everyday example: A student completes assignments on time and receives encouraging feedback. The feedback may increase the likelihood of completing future assignments on time.

Reinforcement and punishment

ConceptWhat happens?Example
Positive reinforcementAdd something desirable to increase behaviour.Giving praise when a student participates constructively.
Negative reinforcementRemove something unpleasant to increase behaviour.A reminder alarm stops after the student submits the task.
Positive punishmentAdd an unpleasant consequence to reduce behaviour.Assigning an additional corrective task after repeated rule-breaking, where appropriate and fair.
Negative punishmentRemove something desirable to reduce behaviour.Temporarily losing a privilege after breaking an agreed rule.

Remember: In psychology, positive means adding something and negative means removing something. Reinforcement increases behaviour; punishment aims to decrease behaviour.

ഓർക്കാൻ എളുപ്പം: Reinforcement പെരുമാറ്റം വർധിപ്പിക്കുന്നു. Punishment പെരുമാറ്റം കുറയ്ക്കാൻ ശ്രമിക്കുന്നു. Positive എന്നത് എന്തെങ്കിലും ചേർക്കുക; Negative എന്നത് എന്തെങ്കിലും നീക്കം ചെയ്യുക എന്നാണ്.

Schedules of reinforcement

  • Continuous: Reinforcement follows every correct response.
  • Fixed ratio: Reinforcement follows a set number of responses.
  • Variable ratio: Reinforcement follows an unpredictable number of responses.
  • Fixed interval: Reinforcement becomes available after a set time period.
  • Variable interval: Reinforcement becomes available after changing, unpredictable time periods.

6. Observational learning — Albert Bandura

Meaning: Observational learning occurs when a person learns by watching another person's behaviour and its consequences.

Everyday example: A first-year student observes how a senior student presents a seminar confidently. The junior student later uses some of the same presentation behaviours.

Bandura's Bobo doll research demonstrated that children can learn aggressive actions by observing an adult model. The study is commonly discussed when teaching observational learning.

Four processes

  1. Attention: The learner notices the model's behaviour.
  2. Retention: The learner remembers what was observed.
  3. Reproduction: The learner has the ability to perform the behaviour.
  4. Motivation: The learner has a reason or incentive to perform it.
ലളിതമായി: മറ്റൊരാൾ ചെയ്യുന്നത് ശ്രദ്ധിച്ച് കാണുകയും ഓർത്തുവെക്കുകയും അവസരം ലഭിക്കുമ്പോൾ അതുപോലെ ചെയ്യുകയും ചെയ്യുന്നതാണ് Observational Learning.

7. Determinants of learning

Meaning: Determinants of learning are the factors that influence how effectively a person learns.

1. MotivationInterest and goals encourage effort. Example: a student studies because they want to understand a topic.
2. ReadinessPrior preparation and developmental readiness help a learner understand new material.
3. AttentionFocusing on the lesson helps important information get processed.
4. PracticeRegular, spaced practice helps strengthen skills and recall.
5. Feedback and reinforcementUseful feedback helps learners correct errors and improve.
6. Learning environmentA safe, quiet and supportive environment can make learning easier.
7. Previous knowledgeConnecting new ideas to what is already known supports understanding.
8. Individual differencesAbilities, language, interests, health and learning needs differ across people.
മലയാളത്തിൽ: പഠനത്തെ സ്വാധീനിക്കുന്ന ഘടകങ്ങളെയാണ് Determinants of Learning എന്ന് പറയുന്നത്. പ്രചോദനം, ശ്രദ്ധ, പരിശീലനം, മുൻ അറിവ്, പ്രതികരണം, പഠനാന്തരീക്ഷം എന്നിവ പഠനത്തെ സഹായിക്കാം.

8. Applications in everyday life and education

  • Teaching: Break a difficult topic into smaller steps and provide timely feedback.
  • Study habits: Use spaced revision and practise retrieving information instead of only rereading.
  • Skill development: Demonstrate a skill, let students practise and provide constructive feedback.
  • Classroom behaviour: Reinforce helpful participation with specific, respectful feedback.
  • Health habits: Use reminders, routines and supportive cues to build consistent behaviours.
Classroom activity — Learn by doing: Ask students to learn a simple paper-folding pattern from a demonstration. First, let them observe; next, ask them to reproduce it; finally, discuss attention, retention, practice and feedback.
